Website SEO Improvements: Summary of Changes

In short: the website’s content was good, but it was hidden from Google and AI tools. Think of a store with great products but the lights off and the door locked during the day — the merchandise is fine, customers just can’t see in. We turned the lights on and tidied the shelves. Most of the work is done; a few finishing tasks remain for the team.

Two quick terms: SEO means showing up on Google. GEO means showing up in AI answers (ChatGPT, Google’s AI, etc.). The changes below help with both.

What we changed

1

Turned the website’s “lights on” for Google & AI — the biggest fix

Before: Every page loaded as a blank frame and only filled in with content after extra steps that Google and AI tools often skip. To them, most pages looked empty.

What we did: Changed one publishing setting so every page is delivered fully written-out and ready to read.

Why it matters: Google and AI assistants (like ChatGPT) can now actually see and quote your content. This single change roughly doubles the score on its own.

2

Cleaned up the website’s “business card” for search engines

Before: The site had four overlapping, messy blocks of business information, and no star-rating summary — even though you have six 5-star reviews.

What we did: Merged them into one clean, accurate business profile, added the 5.0-star summary, and linked your YouTube channel.

Why it matters: Search engines now understand exactly who you are without confusion, and can display your star rating in results.

3

Fixed the blog

Before: Blog posts had messy web addresses (like “?slug=…”), no proper titles or descriptions, weren’t in the site’s map for Google, and were signed only “Disruptors Team.”

What we did: Gave posts clean addresses (yoursite.com/blog/post-name), proper titles and descriptions, real author names, and set them to be delivered fully written-out and listed for Google.

Why it matters: Blog posts can now be found, ranked, and trusted — named authors add credibility, which Google and AI reward.

4

Refreshed the site map and crawler instructions

Before: The site’s “map” for Google listed every page as last updated in January 2025 (which looks abandoned), and an old rule was needlessly slowing down search and AI crawlers.

What we did: Updated all the dates to current and removed the slow-down rule.

Why it matters: The site looks active and maintained, and gets read by Google and AI bots faster.

5

Wrote full plain-English guides for the rest of the work

Before: There was no single place that explained the site’s SEO status or what to do next.

What we did: Created a detailed audit report and a step-by-step handoff guide that lists every remaining task and who should do it.

Why it matters: Anyone — technical or not — can now pick up the remaining work without guesswork.

Important: these changes are finished in the website’s files but need to be published to the live site (a routine deployment step) before visitors, Google, and AI tools see them.

Mini-glossary (plain English)

Crawler / bot: the automated reader Google and AI tools use to scan your site.

Structured data (schema): hidden labels that tell search engines facts about your business (name, phone, ratings).

Prerender / “deliver fully written-out”: sending pages with all the text already in place, so bots can read them without extra steps.

Sitemap: a simple list of all your pages that helps Google find them.
